The Solar System Overview

The Solar System is the gravitationally bound system of the Sun and the objects
that orbit it. It formed approximately 4.6 billion years ago from the gravitational
collapse of a giant interstellar molecular cloud.

The Sun
The Sun is a G-type main-sequence star at the center of the Solar System. It
contains 99.86% of the system's known mass. Energy from the Sun is produced by
nuclear fusion of hydrogen into helium in its core.

The Inner Planets
The four inner planets are Mercury, Venus, Earth, and Mars. They are also called
terrestrial planets because they have rocky surfaces. Earth is the only planet
known to support life and has one natural satellite, the Moon.

The Outer Planets
The four outer planets are Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, and Neptune. Jupiter and
Saturn are gas giants composed mainly of hydrogen and helium. Uranus and Neptune
are ice giants and contain larger amounts of water, methane, and ammonia.

Dwarf Planets and Small Bodies
Pluto, Eris, Haumea, Makemake, and Ceres are classified as dwarf planets.
The asteroid belt lies between Mars and Jupiter and contains millions of rocky
objects. The Kuiper Belt and the Oort Cloud lie beyond Neptune and contain
icy bodies, including most known comets.
